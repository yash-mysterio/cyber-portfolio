Incident Management on the Power Platform

A small system for the unglamorous half of incident response: recording an incident, deciding how serious it is, making sure the right people are told, and seeing the overall picture. Built as an app, an automation layer and a set of dashboards.

00 / Scope

Built as a personal project, not as an organisational deployment. This page describes what the application does and the thinking behind its design, rather than how it performed against real incidents.

01 / The problem

Four questions every incident raises

Incident response comes down to a few basic questions: what happened, how serious is it, who needs to know, and is anyone dealing with it. When the answers live in email threads and memory, incidents get lost, severity gets argued rather than recorded, and nobody can see the overall picture. This project puts those answers into one small system.

02 / The design

Three tools, one job each

01 · POWER APPS

Submit, track and triage

An application where cybersecurity incidents are submitted, tracked and triaged.

02 · POWER AUTOMATE

Notify and escalate

Automated workflows that send notifications based on severity and escalate incidents.

03 · POWER BI

Report

Dashboards showing incident status, severity and trends.

The split matters. The app is where information enters and is kept. The automation acts on it, so that severity rather than memory decides who gets told. The dashboards turn the accumulated records into a picture of what is open, how serious it is, and which way it's moving.

03 / Severity

The field everything hangs on

Severity is the field the rest of the system depends on. It determines how quickly someone needs to know, who needs to know, and how an incident ranks during triage. Recording it as structured data and acting on it automatically is what makes triage consistent, instead of dependent on whoever happens to be looking.

04 / Access control

A dashboard is also a disclosure risk

Incident records are sensitive. They can describe vulnerabilities, affected systems and the people involved, so a dashboard that shows everyone everything creates its own risk. The dashboards were built with access control for that data in mind.

It's also the thread running through my SAP work at Accenture, where who could see and do what was the job.

05 / Where it fits

The response side of the gap

In my governance analysis of unpatchable devices, the sharpest gap was that a detector without a response path is an alarm nobody is required to answer. This project sits on the response side of that gap: somewhere for an incident to be recorded, prioritised and escalated. The incident command structure I wrote for the business continuity plan covers who decides; this covers where the information lives.
